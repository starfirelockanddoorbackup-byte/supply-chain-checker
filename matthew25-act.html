Federal Legislative Framework

The Matthew 25 Supply Chain Accountability Act

Targeting forced labor loopholes, abusive import wage tariffs, and corporate margin extraction while establishing strict transparency standards for multinational supply chains.

Executive Summary

Named in alignment with principles of human dignity and stewardship, the Matthew 25 Supply Chain Accountability Act creates a statutory mandate forcing corporations importing goods into domestic markets to prove absolute transparency regarding worker compensation, living wage compliance, and human rights audits.

Core Legislative Provisions

  • 1. Elimination of Forced Labor Import Loopholes Closes enforcement gaps in historical trade acts by shifting the burden of proof onto importing corporations to certify zero forced or coerced labor across all tier-3 and tier-4 sub-suppliers.
  • 2. Living Wage Benchmark Tariffs Applies escalating import tariffs on consumer goods where primary assembly and raw material harvesting wages fall below regional living wage benchmarks, neutralizing the artificial cost advantage gained through worker exploitation.
  • 3. Public Margin & Wage Transparency Disclosure Mandates that large multinational enterprises publish a standardized "Value Distribution Index" on product packaging and digital storefronts, showing the exact ratio of corporate executive margins versus direct assembly wages.

Impact Projections

Economic modeling indicates that tying import tariffs to living wage compliance lifts millions of international assembly workers out of extreme poverty while incentivizing domestic manufacturing and resilient regional supply chains.
